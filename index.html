Young men are expected to fix things, stay safe online and handle money. Many were never shown how.

A video shows the steps, but it can’t tell you what you did wrong or when to stop. Parents working long hours can’t be there for every little lesson, and that’s not their fault. And boys are often told to tough it out, so they figure things out alone.

Why we started

1 of 5

We’re Dane, Jewell and Marco, three college students who kept getting asked to fix phones, paint walls, fix cabinets and deal with car trouble. Usually nobody had shown us how.

This isn’t about parents who didn’t care. It’s about families doing everything they can, and kids who just need someone to take the time to teach them.

Good to know

Are workshops running yet?
Not yet. Own Two Feet is a student venture that’s still in development, so no workshops are running or booking. Join the interest list and we’ll email you when we launch.
Who are the workshops for?
Boys and young men in elementary and middle school. Activities are matched to their age. Topics for older students, like car basics, come later.
Couldn’t students just watch a video?
Videos can show the steps. In a workshop, a real person explains why, fixes mistakes right away, and talks about when to stop.
How do you keep sessions safe?
Everything is supervised and right for their age. Students never work on electrical, gas or other dangerous repairs, and every topic covers when to get an adult or a pro.
What will it cost?
We plan to charge $350 for a 50-minute workshop and $500 for a 90-minute one, per session, for up to 30 students. We bring the supplies. These are planned prices, and nothing is booking yet.
Will there be manuals?
Yes. We’re writing a free manual for each topic that teachers, parents and students can use at home or in class. They’re not ready yet. The three activities on this site are free to try now.

We had to figure it out alone. They shouldn’t have to.

Own Two Feet started with three students, Dane, Jewell and Marco, comparing notes. We’d all been the person people turned to for a frozen laptop, a cabinet off its hinge, a wall that needed paint, or a car that wouldn’t start. Most of the time, nobody had shown us how.

Some of us grew up without a dad around much. All of us watched parents and relatives work long hours to keep things going. A busy parent isn’t a missing parent. Sometimes everyone’s just busy, and a kid steps up and figures it out alone.

So we asked a simple question: how many other boys are in the same spot? The problem isn’t that they can’t learn. It’s that someone may never have had the time to teach them.

Still building

Own Two Feet is a student venture. We’re writing our first workshops and manuals now. No workshops are running yet, and we’re not booking any.

Our interviews shaped the plan

We talked to parents, mentors and young men about growing up and learning this stuff.

Videos help, but they don’t stick

Stuff you learn alone from a video is easy to forget. Learning from a real person sticks.

Kids don’t want to bother busy parents

A lot of boys figure things out on their own so they don’t add to their parents’ stress.

Asking for help is hard

Workshops have to make questions feel normal and safe.

Not everyone thinks they need it

So we focus on what free videos can’t give you: hands-on practice with someone there to help.

Four commitments

Support families, never replace them

We work alongside parents, counselors and mentors, and send skills home with students.

Practice beats watching

Students learn by doing, with someone nearby to answer questions and catch mistakes.

Know your limits

Knowing when to stop and ask for help is a skill too, and we teach it every session.

Confidence, not judgment

No student is behind because of how his family works. Everyone starts somewhere.
