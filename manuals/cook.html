For grown-ups

This manual is written to the student. Read it together the first time, then let them lead. Every lesson ends with a red box that says when to stop. The stove, the oven, sharp knives, hot oil and anything hot from the microwave are adult-check items, and an adult should be in the kitchen for all of them. The recipes are simple on purpose: a sandwich, scrambled eggs, oatmeal and a quesadilla. Skill levels differ a lot between a third grader and an eighth grader, so let each student do what they can do safely and step in for the rest. We link every source at the bottom.

Clean start

Handwashing, a clean surface and safe knife handling.

Why it matters

A kitchen is a fun place to make something real. It is also a place with heat, sharp tools and germs you cannot see. A few habits keep you safe and keep the food safe to eat.1 Good cooks do these habits every single time, even the pros. Nobody is born knowing them.

Get ready

  1. Ask first. Tell an adult you want to cook, and ask them to be nearby.4
  2. Dress for it. Put on clean clothes, tie back long hair and roll up long sleeves so they stay away from heat.46
  3. Wash your hands. Use soap and water and scrub for at least 20 seconds.2 Wash again during cooking if you touch raw egg or raw meat, and again when you finish.2
  4. Start with a clean surface. Wipe the counter and use clean tools and a clean cutting board.4
  5. Read the whole recipe. Then gather every ingredient and tool before you start.4

Four habits of a safe kitchen

Clean

Wash hands and surfaces often. Wash cutting boards, tools and counters with hot, soapy water.1

Separate

Keep raw eggs and raw meat away from ready-to-eat foods like bread and fruit. Use a different plate or board for each.1

Cook

Cook food all the way. Color is not a reliable way to tell, so an adult uses a food thermometer.1

Chill

Put food that can spoil in the refrigerator soon after you finish.1

Knives, the safe way

Knife skills take practice, and you start small. Begin with a plastic knife on soft foods like a banana or cheese, and move up as your skill grows, with an adult showing you how.5

  1. Set up the board. Put a damp towel under the cutting board so it does not slide.5
  2. Curl your fingers. Hold the food with your fingertips tucked in, like a claw, so they stay away from the blade.5
  3. Cut slowly. Keep your eyes on the blade. Never point a knife at anyone.5
  4. Put it down safely. Set the knife on the board, away from the edge, and tell people nearby where it is.
Hot stuff. Use a dry oven mitt or potholder, not a towel, because a towel can fall onto a burner and catch fire.6 Turn pot and pan handles toward the back of the stove.6 Keep a kid-free zone three feet around the stove and oven.6

Try it at home

Hand-wash timer. Wash your hands while you sing a song that lasts 20 seconds. Count how many times you need to sing it.

Kitchen check. Walk through the kitchen with an adult. Find the pot holders, the way out of the kitchen and the place where sharp knives are kept.

Practice the claw. With an adult and a plastic knife, slice a banana using the curled-fingers grip.

Stop and get an adult

  • You want to use the stove, the oven or a sharp knife. An adult must be with you.
  • You smell smoke or see flames. Get away from the stove and tell an adult right away.
  • Something spills on the floor and you could slip. Tell an adult and wipe it up with them.
  • You get a cut or a burn. Tell an adult right away.
  • You are not sure how to use a tool or an appliance. Ask. Guessing is not a plan.

Read a recipe

Measuring, timing and following steps in order.

Why it matters

A recipe is a set of instructions from someone who already made the dish. If you follow it in order, you can make food you have never made before. Kids your age can read recipe directions and follow the steps, with an adult close by for the tricky parts.3

How to read a recipe

  1. Read it all the way through first. Read the ingredients, the tools and every step before you touch anything.4
  2. Gather everything. Put every ingredient and tool on the counter. If something is missing, you find out now, not halfway through.4
  3. Check for the grown-up steps. Circle any step with a stove, oven, knife or hot oil. That is where your adult helps.
  4. Follow the steps in order. Tick each one off when you finish it.
  5. Watch the clock. Use a timer for anything that cooks, so you are not guessing.

Measuring

Dry ingredients

Scoop a little extra into the cup, then slide a flat edge across the top to level it off.5

Liquids

Use a clear measuring cup on a flat counter. Bend down so your eyes are level with the line, then read it.5

Spoons

A tablespoon is bigger than a teaspoon. Check which one the recipe says, because mixing them up changes the food.

Four simple recipes

Each one is a good first meal. The no-stove one comes first.

Turkey and cheese sandwich

No stove. Wash your hands. Lay out two slices of bread, add cheese, turkey and a little lettuce, and close it up. Slice it with a plastic knife. Wipe the counter when you are done.

Scrambled eggs

Adult at the stove. Crack two eggs in a bowl and whisk them with a fork. Wash your hands. Your adult heats the pan and you stir slowly until the eggs are firm, with no runny parts.

Oatmeal

Adult with hot water. Measure the oats and the liquid with a measuring cup. Your adult handles the stove or microwave and the hot bowl. Let it cool a bit, then stir in fruit.

Cheese quesadilla

Adult at the pan. Sprinkle cheese on one half of a tortilla and fold it over. Your adult heats it in the pan and flips it. Let it cool before you cut it.

Raw egg is for cooking, not tasting. Do not eat raw cookie dough or batter, because it can have raw eggs in it.7 Cook egg dishes all the way, and wash your hands after you crack eggs.7

Try it at home

Recipe read-through. Pick a recipe with an adult. Read it out loud, then list every ingredient and tool.

Measure race. Practice with water and a clear cup. Can you hit one cup exactly? Now try half a cup.

Make the sandwich. Follow the sandwich recipe from start to finish. Read, gather, make, clean.

Stop and get an adult

  • Any step that uses the stove, the oven or hot oil. Do not turn on a burner by yourself.
  • Any step that needs a sharp knife. Ask your adult to show you first.
  • Anything hot coming out of the microwave. Open lids away from your face so the steam escapes, and use potholders.7
  • Never put metal, aluminum foil or metal containers in the microwave. Use glass or a dish marked for microwave use, and ask an adult first.7
  • The recipe has a word or a tool you do not know. Ask before you guess.

Make it, clean it

A simple meal, then cleanup as part of cooking.

Why it matters

Cooking is not finished when the food is on the plate. It is finished when the kitchen is clean again. Cleaning up keeps germs from spreading and makes the next cook, maybe you, glad to walk in.1

Make the meal

  1. Start clean. Wash your hands and wipe the counter.2
  2. Do your prep. Measure, crack, mix and assemble. Keep raw egg away from bread, fruit and anything you will eat without cooking.1
  3. Bring in your adult for heat. Anything on the stove, in the oven or in hot oil is their job, with you beside them.
  4. Let hot food cool. Hot food and hot steam cause bad burns. Wait a minute, then taste.7
  5. Sit down and enjoy it. You made it.

Clean it up

  1. Wipe spills right away. A wet floor is a slip risk.4
  2. Wash the dishes and tools. Use hot, soapy water for cutting boards, tools and counters after each food.1
  3. Wash knives by hand. Wash them one at a time with the blade pointing away from you, and never leave one hiding in a sink full of suds.5
  4. Wipe the counters. Do the surface where raw egg or meat touched last.3
  5. Put food away. Food that can spoil goes in the refrigerator soon. If it has been out more than two hours, it is time to throw it away.1
  6. Wash your hands again. Then check the stove and oven are off.

Mistakes happen

Burnt toast, a runny egg, flour on the floor. Every cook has done all of them. Fix it, learn from it and try again. A messy first try is still a first try.

Try it at home

Make a meal and clean it. Make a sandwich, then clean the counter, wash the plate and put every ingredient away. Time yourself.

Breakfast helper. With an adult at the stove, make scrambled eggs or oatmeal for your family.

Leftover rule. Check the clock when food comes off the table. Put the leftovers away before two hours pass.1

Stop and get an adult

  • A pan of oil or grease catches fire. Do not use water. Get away and tell an adult right away.6
  • Fire in the oven. Keep the door closed, and tell an adult.6
  • A pan, pot or dish is still hot. Leave it for your adult to move and clean.
  • You broke glass. Do not pick it up with bare hands. Tell an adult.
  • You think food was left out too long, smells strange or looks off. Ask before eating it.1
  • Someone gets sick after a meal, or you get a burn or a cut. Tell an adult.

Did it stick?

Answer out loud, then tap to see.

How long should you scrub your hands before you cook?

At least 20 seconds, with soap and water.

What do you do first when you start a recipe?

Read the whole recipe, then gather every ingredient and tool before you begin.

How do you hold food while you cut it?

With your fingertips curled in, like a claw, so they stay away from the blade. An adult stays close by.

Why should you use a pot holder and not a towel?

A towel can fall onto a burner and catch fire.

Which cooking jobs need an adult standing next to you?

The stove, the oven, sharp knives and hot oil. Hot things from the microwave too.

Can you put aluminum foil or a metal bowl in the microwave?

No. Use glass or a dish marked for microwave use, and ask an adult first.

A pan of oil catches fire. What do you do?

Do not use water. Get away from the stove and tell an adult right away.

How long can leftovers sit out before they go in the fridge?

No more than two hours. After that, they should be thrown away.
