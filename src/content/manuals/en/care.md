      <span class="num">Lesson 1</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Read the label</h2>
      <p class="sub">What the wash and dry symbols mean.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Every piece of clothing comes with instructions. Following them can be the difference between a shirt that lasts and one that shrinks, fades or gets ruined.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Nobody is born knowing this. Once you can read the label, you can wash your own clothes with confidence.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Where to find it and what it says</h3>
      <p>The care label is a small tag sewn inside the clothing. Look at the inside of the collar, the neckline or the waist seam. Sometimes it is on another inside seam.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Leave it attached, so you always know how to care for that piece.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
      <p>The picture symbols look like a secret code at first. They tell you which cycle to use, how to dry the item and what heat to use if you iron it.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Here is how to crack the code.</p>

      <div class="tools">
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>The tub is washing</h4><p>A picture of a wash tub means washing. Dots inside mean heat: one dot is cool, more dots are hotter. Lines underneath mean go gentle.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>The square is drying</h4><p>A square means drying. A circle inside the square means the dryer. Dots again show the heat. A line or a hanger means dry it flat or hang it up.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>The triangle is bleach</h4><p>An empty triangle means bleach is okay. A triangle with an X means no bleach at all. Either way, bleach is an adult job.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>The iron is ironing</h4><p>An iron with dots shows how hot it can go. An X through it means do not iron. Ask an adult before you touch an iron.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>An X means no</h4><p>Any symbol with an X through it means do not do that to this piece of clothing.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>A circle is dry cleaning</h4><p>A plain circle means the item is for a dry cleaner. Do not put it in your home washer. Hand it to an adult.</p></div></div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="h3">How to read a label</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Find the tag.</b> Check the collar, neckline and waist seam first.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Check the tub.</b> What temperature does it show? Is there a line under it?</li>
        <li><b>Check the X marks.</b> Anything crossed out is a no.</li>
        <li><b>Check the square.</b> Dryer, or hang it up? Some items shrink in the dryer, so the label matters.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Say it out loud.</b> “Wash cool, no bleach, hang to dry.” If you can say it, you can do it.</li>
      </ol>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>Not sure? Ask.</b> If a symbol looks new to you, do not guess. Show an adult, or wash the item the gentlest way and wait to learn more. Never cut the tag out, or you lose the instructions.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></div></div>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Label hunt.</b> Find the care label on five things you wear. Which ones say cool water? Which ones say do not put in the dryer?</p>
        <p><b>Symbol match.</b> Draw the tub, the square, the triangle and the iron on index cards. Mix them up, then say what each one means.</p>
        <p><b>Make a pile.</b> Sort five clothes into “dryer okay” and “hang to dry.”</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>The label shows a bleach triangle, or the item is stained and you want to use bleach or a strong cleaner. Ask first. Never mix cleaning products.</li>
          <li>The label says dry clean only, or you cannot read the symbols.</li>
          <li>You want to use an iron or hot water. Check with an adult.</li>
          <li>There is no label at all and you are not sure how to wash it.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 2</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Run a load</h2>
      <p class="sub">Sort, measure, wash, dry and fold.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Clean clothes are part of feeling ready for the day. Doing your own laundry is also a skill you will use every week for the rest of your life. A load takes a few steps, and once you know them, it is easy.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">The steps</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Check pockets and close up.</b> Empty every pocket. Shake off loose dirt and close zippers and hooks.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Sort.</b> Whites go with whites, and lights go apart from darks. Wash towels and other fabrics that shed lint apart from the ones that attract it. Wash very dirty clothes apart from lightly dirty ones. Sorting stops colors from running onto other clothes.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Pick the temperature.</b> Check the care label first. Cold is good for dark and bright colors that might bleed. Warm works for most clothes. Hot is for towels, bedding and very dirty items, but it can shrink or fade some fabrics.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Check with an adult before you choose hot.</li>
        <li><b>Measure the detergent.</b> Every load needs detergent. Follow the amounts on the bottle and measure before you add it. A very small load needs less, and a very big load may need more. Use detergent marked HE if the washer is a high-efficiency one.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Load it loosely.</b> Do not pack the clothes in. Packing leaves detergent on clothes and makes extra lint.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Start the wash.</b> Pick the cycle that fits the label and press start.</li>
        <li><b>Move to the dryer, or hang it.</b> Shake damp clothes out first. Items the label says to hang go on a rack or flat. Do not overload the dryer.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Clean the lint filter.</b> Clean the lint filter before or after each use, and never run the dryer without one.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Ask an adult to check it with you the first time.</li>
        <li><b>Take clothes out when it stops.</b> Taking them out right away means fewer wrinkles.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Fold and put away.</b> Fold shirts and pants flat, pair the socks and put each pile where it lives. Do it while the clothes are warm and the wrinkles are easy to smooth.</li>
      </ol>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>The dryer needs care.</b> The leading cause of home dryer fires is not cleaning the dryer.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Do not leave a dryer running if you leave home or go to bed.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Never dry anything that has touched cooking oil, gasoline, paint thinner or alcohol.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup></div></div>

      <div class="panel sand box">
        <h3 class="h3">Laundry packets are not toys</h3>
        <p>Single-load detergent packets can look like candy. Store them in their original container, out of sight and reach of young children, and do not let children handle them.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> If you have a little brother or sister at home, this one is on you. Put the container back up high every time.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Sorting race.</b> With a basket of clean clothes, sort them into whites, lights and darks. How fast can you do it?</p>
        <p><b>Run one load.</b> With an adult next to you, run a small load start to finish. Measure the detergent yourself.</p>
        <p><b>Fold challenge.</b> Fold five shirts and pair five socks. Teach someone else how.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>Before you use bleach, a stain remover or any strong cleaning product. Ask first, and never mix products.</li>
          <li>Before you pick hot water or use an iron.</li>
          <li>Lint that has built up around the dryer, a dryer that takes much longer than normal, or a burning smell. Turn it off and tell an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup></li>
          <li>Clothes with cooking oil, gasoline, paint thinner or alcohol on them. Do not put them in the dryer.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup></li>
          <li>A laundry packet or detergent that a young child touched or swallowed. Tell an adult right away. They can call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222.</li>
          <li>Water on the floor, strange noises or a smell from the machine. Stop it and tell an adult.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 3</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Everyday routines</h2>
      <p class="sub">Simple habits that stick: showering, deodorant, teeth, hair, nails, clean clothes and sleep.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>As you get older, your body changes. You may sweat more and notice new smells. That is normal, and it happens to everyone. A few simple habits help you feel fresh, healthy and ready for the day. Small habits you do every day beat big efforts you do once in a while.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Build a routine</h3>
      <div class="tools">
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Shower or wash up</h4><p>Wash your body with soap and water every day or two, and after sports or heavy sweating. Pay attention to underarms, feet and your private areas. Dry well, especially between your toes.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Deodorant</h4><p>Use it on clean, dry underarms after you shower. Ask an adult to help you pick one that does not bother your skin.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Teeth</h4><p>Brush twice a day with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup> Aim for two minutes each time. Floss once a day.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> Replace your toothbrush every three or four months, or sooner if the bristles are frayed.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup></p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Hands</h4><p>Wash your hands with soap and water, and scrub for at least 20 seconds. Do it before you eat or cook and after the bathroom.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Hair</h4><p>Wash it with shampoo a few times a week, or when it feels oily or dirty. Comb or brush it before you go out.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Nails</h4><p>Keep fingernails short and clean. Trim them after a shower when they are soft. Cut straight across and use clippers made for nails.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Clean clothes</h4><p>Put on clean underwear and socks every day. Wear a shirt again only if it still looks and smells fresh. Put dirty clothes in the hamper, not on the floor.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Sleep</h4><p>Kids ages 6 to 12 need 9 to 12 hours of sleep a day, and teens 13 to 17 need 8 to 10.<sup class="c"><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> Pick a bedtime and a short wind-down, like a shower, brushing and a book.</p></div></div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="h3">Make it stick</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Start with one habit.</b> Pick the one that feels easiest, and do it for two weeks.</li>
        <li><b>Tie it to something you already do.</b> Brush your teeth right after breakfast. Put on deodorant right after your shower.</li>
        <li><b>Make it easy.</b> Keep your toothbrush, soap and deodorant where you can see them.</li>
        <li><b>Use a checklist.</b> Tick it off each day. Seeing a row of checks feels great.</li>
        <li><b>Add the next habit.</b> Build the routine one piece at a time.</li>
      </ol>

      <div class="panel mist box">
        <h3 class="h3">Everyone starts somewhere</h3>
        <p>You do not need to be perfect. Missing a day is fine. Start again the next day. Every person you know has had to learn these habits, and the people who care about you will be glad you are working on them.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Make a morning card.</b> Write your morning routine in order and tape it where you will see it.</p>
        <p><b>Two-minute timer.</b> Brush for two minutes by a timer or a favorite song.</p>
        <p><b>Pack a go bag.</b> Put deodorant, a clean shirt and socks in your sports bag.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>Skin that is red, sore, itchy or has a rash, or a smell that does not go away with washing. A doctor can help.</li>
          <li>A tooth that hurts, or gums that bleed a lot.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></li>
          <li>You get a cut while trimming your nails, or you need to use sharp tools such as razors. Ask for help.</li>
          <li>Hot water that feels too hot. Turn it down or ask an adult to adjust it.</li>
          <li>Trouble sleeping for a long time, or feeling very tired or worried. Tell an adult you trust.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
