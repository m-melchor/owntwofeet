      <span class="num">Lesson 1</span>
      <h2 class="h2">First impressions</h2>
      <p class="sub">A handshake, eye contact and a clear introduction.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Meeting someone new is easier when you know what to say. A clear hello tells the other person who you are, and it helps you feel steadier too. Good talking is also good listening. Listening means paying attention to the other person, not just waiting for your turn.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></p>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>Shy is fine.</b> You do not have to be loud or outgoing. Many people feel nervous meeting someone new. Your accent is fine too, and every family greets people in its own way. Pick the parts that feel right for you and practice them a little at a time.</div></div>

      <h3 class="h3">Say hello</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Look up and find a friendly face.</b> Eye contact shows you are paying attention. It is a skill you can build a little at a time, so if it feels hard, start with just a quick look.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> Looking at the person’s face, or near their eyes, works too.</li>
        <li><b>Say your name clearly.</b> “Hi, I’m Marcus.” Say it slowly enough that they can catch it. If they do not hear you, it is okay to say it again.</li>
        <li><b>Greet them your way.</b> A handshake, a wave or a smile all work. For a handshake, hold out your hand, give a gentle squeeze and shake once or twice. If you do not want to shake hands, a wave and a hello are just as polite.</li>
        <li><b>Ask one question.</b> “How are you?” or “What’s your name?” Then listen to the answer.</li>
        <li><b>Use their name.</b> Try to say it back once: “Nice to meet you, Ms. Lopez.” It helps you remember it.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">Listen like you mean it</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Face the person.</b> Put your phone and other things down.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Let them finish.</b> Wait until they stop talking before you answer.</li>
        <li><b>Say it back in your own words.</b> “So you coach the soccer team?” That shows you heard them.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Ask a question that needs more than yes or no.</b> “What do you like about it?” keeps the talk going.<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Mirror hello.</b> Stand in front of a mirror and say your name three times. Slow and clear wins.</p>
        <p><b>Pretend party.</b> Have a family member pretend to be someone new, like a coach or a neighbor. Practice a hello, your name and one question.</p>
        <p><b>Listening game.</b> Ask someone about their day. When they finish, say back one thing you heard.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>An adult or stranger makes you uncomfortable, asks you to keep a secret, or asks you to go somewhere with them. Walk away and tell a trusted adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
          <li>Someone asks for your home address, your phone number or other private details. Do not answer. Tell an adult.</li>
          <li>Someone does not take no for an answer when you do not want a handshake or a hug. Step back and get an adult.</li>
          <li>Someone is being hurt or threatened. Get an adult right away.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 2</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Make the call</h2>
      <p class="sub">Planning what to say, then calling to ask or reschedule.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Sooner or later you will need to call someone: a coach, a doctor’s office, a store or a relative. A call feels much easier when you plan it first. And one call is special. Everyone should know how to call 911 and what to say, and also when it is not the right number.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup><sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></p>

      <h3 class="h3">Plan your call</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Know why you are calling.</b> Write one sentence: “I want to ask what time practice starts.”</li>
        <li><b>Write down what you need.</b> The names, the day, the time and your question. Keep a pencil handy for the answer.</li>
        <li><b>Write a short opening.</b> “Hello, my name is Jordan. May I speak with Coach Reyes?”</li>
        <li><b>Practice it out loud once.</b> Speak slowly and clearly.</li>
        <li><b>End it kindly.</b> Say thank you and goodbye. Wait a moment before you hang up.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">Leaving a voicemail</h3>
      <p>Sometimes no one answers, and that is normal. Wait for the beep, then say:</p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Who you are.</b> “Hi, this is Jordan.”</li>
        <li><b>Why you called.</b> “I’m calling to ask about Saturday’s game.”</li>
        <li><b>How to reach you.</b> Say your phone number slowly. Say it twice if you can.</li>
        <li><b>Say thanks.</b> Then goodbye. Keep the whole message short.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">Calling a business</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Say hello and ask for what you need.</b> “Hello, I’m calling to ask what time you close today.”</li>
        <li><b>Have an adult close by.</b> Ask them to listen in the first few times.</li>
        <li><b>Do not give out private information.</b> No card numbers, no passwords. If they ask for something like that, give the phone to your adult.</li>
        <li><b>Rescheduling?</b> Say what you need to change: “I have an appointment Tuesday at 4. Can we move it to Wednesday?” Write down the new day and time.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">What 911 is for</h3>
      <p>911 is for emergencies. An emergency is a time when something serious happens and immediate help is needed.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Call 911 when someone is badly hurt, when someone will not wake up, or when you need the police or the fire department.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Stay safe and stay calm.</b> Take a deep breath. It helps you think clearly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Call 911.</b> Speak slowly and clearly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Tell them what is happening.</b> Say what the emergency is.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Give the address.</b> At home, say your address. Away from home, give the address or a nearby landmark.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup><sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Stay on the line.</b> Do not hang up until the operator says it is okay.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>What 911 is not for.</b> Do not call 911 for a small cut or bruise you can take care of at home, a limping cat, or a ride to soccer practice.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> If you dial 911 by accident, do not hang up. Stay on the line and say what happened. If you hang up, they will need to send someone to check on you.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup><sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></div></div>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Script it.</b> Pick a pretend call, like asking a store when it opens. Write your opening and your question, then call a family member and practice.</p>
        <p><b>Voicemail practice.</b> Say a voicemail out loud to a family member. Did they hear your name and your number?</p>
        <p><b>Pretend 911.</b> With an adult, role-play a 911 call. One of you is the operator. Do not use a real phone for this.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
        <p><a class="btn btn-line" href="../#call">Practice with Make the Call<svg class="arr" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M5 12h14M13 6l6 6-6 6"/></svg></a></p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>A caller asks for your name, your address, your card numbers, a password or when you are home alone. Do not answer. Hang up and tell an adult.</li>
          <li>Someone on the phone makes you uncomfortable or asks you to keep the call a secret. Hang up and tell an adult.</li>
          <li>Someone is hurt, in danger or will not wake up. Get an adult and call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>You are not sure if it is an emergency. Ask an adult. If no adult is near and someone may be in danger, call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
          <li>You dialed 911 by accident. Stay on the line, then tell an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 3</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Disagree well</h2>
      <p class="sub">Staying calm and respectful when you don’t agree.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Everyone disagrees sometimes, with friends, brothers and sisters, and adults. Disagreeing is not bad. What matters is how you do it. When you share how you feel without blaming, the other person can listen more calmly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup></p>

      <h3 class="h3">Say it with an I statement</h3>
      <p>An I statement tells how you feel without blaming the other person.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup> A simple pattern is: “I feel ___ when ___. Can we ___?”</p>
      <div class="tools">
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Instead of this</h4><p>“You always take my turn! You’re so unfair!”</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>Try this</h4><p>“I feel upset when I don’t get a turn. Can we take turns?”</p></div></div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="h3">Steps for a disagreement</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Pause and breathe.</b> Take a deep breath before you answer. Staying calm helps you think clearly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Listen first.</b> Let the other person finish. Then say back what you heard: “So you want to play the other game because it’s your turn to pick?”<sup class="c"><a href="#s6">6</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Say your side with an I statement.</b> Keep your voice calm and your words short.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Look for a middle.</b> Offer a few ideas, like taking turns, doing one each, or asking a grown-up to help decide.</li>
        <li><b>It is okay to agree to disagree.</b> You do not have to win. Sometimes people just see it differently.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">When to walk away</h3>
      <p>Some talks do not get better, and that is okay. If you feel too angry to talk calmly, take a break. You can say, “I need a minute,” and go somewhere safe. If someone is teasing you or being mean, tell them to stop in a calm, clear voice. If that does not feel safe or does not work, walk away, stay away from that person and find an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup> Stay near adults and other kids. Walking away is not giving up. It is a smart choice.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></p>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Switch it.</b> Take a “you” sentence like “You never listen!” and rewrite it as an I statement. Say it out loud.</p>
        <p><b>Two sides.</b> With a family member, pick something light to disagree about, like the best pizza topping. Take turns listening and saying it back before you give your side.</p>
        <p><b>Calm-down plan.</b> Write down what helps you calm down, like deep breaths, a drink of water or a quiet spot. Share it with an adult.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>A disagreement turns into shoving, hitting or threats. Walk away and get an adult now.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
          <li>Someone keeps picking on you or leaving you out and it does not stop. That is bullying. Tell a trusted adult. You do not have to keep it inside.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
          <li>You see someone else being bullied or hurt. Tell an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
          <li>An adult or stranger makes you uncomfortable during an argument or tells you to keep it secret. Leave and tell a trusted adult.</li>
          <li>Someone in a chat or on a call asks for personal information to “fix” a fight. Do not answer. Tell an adult.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
