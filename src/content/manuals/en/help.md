      <span class="num">Lesson 1</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Handle, ask or call</h2>
      <p class="sub">Sorting situations by who should handle them.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Every day something comes up. A spill, a stuck zipper, a strange smell, a friend who looks upset. You do not have to do everything yourself, and you do not have to panic either. You just need a quick way to sort it. In an emergency, people sometimes do not help because they are not sure what to do, or they think someone else will.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> If you know how to sort the problem, you can keep yourself and others safe.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>Asking is a skill, not a failure.</b> Smart people ask all the time. Part of staying safe is knowing who to ask, and one skill from FEMA is simply to ask someone you trust to help you.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Asking early often keeps a small problem small.</div></div>

      <h3 class="h3">Three buckets</h3>
      <p>Put every problem in one of three buckets. When you are not sure, go up one bucket.</p>
      <div class="tools">
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>I can handle it</h4><p>It is small, safe and you already know how. Wiping up spilled water. Finding a lost pencil. Tying a shoe. Looking up a word. Cleaning a tiny scrape the way you were taught.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>I ask an adult</h4><p>You are not sure, it needs a tool or a stove you have not been shown, or it just feels off. A job with a ladder. A smell you cannot place. A mean message. A stranger at the door. A friend who is very sad.</p></div></div>
        <div class="tool" style="grid-template-columns:1fr"><div><h4>We call for help</h4><p>Someone is badly hurt or will not wake up. There is fire or smoke. Someone is breaking in. You need the police or the fire department.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Get to a safe place first, then call.</p></div></div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="h3">Sort it in four steps</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Stop and take a breath.</b> Staying calm helps you think more clearly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Ask: is anyone in danger right now?</b> If yes, get safe and use the third bucket. Call for help.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Ask: do I know how to do this safely, and has an adult shown me?</b> If both answers are yes, it may be yours to handle.</li>
        <li><b>Not sure? Ask.</b> Say what you see: “I found something and I am not sure what to do.” Then wait for the answer before you touch it.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">Try sorting these</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>The milk spills on the floor.</b> Handle it. Grab a towel.</li>
        <li><b>Your friend wants you to climb on the roof to get a ball.</b> Ask an adult. Do not climb.</li>
        <li><b>You smell smoke in the kitchen.</b> Leave and call. Get out first, then tell an adult and call 911 from a safe place.</li>
        <li><b>A cut is small and stops bleeding.</b> Handle it with the first aid you know, and tell an adult after.</li>
        <li><b>A cut will not stop bleeding.</b> Ask an adult right now, and call 911 if no adult is near.</li>
      </ol>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Bucket game.</b> Write ten everyday problems on paper slips. Sort them into three piles labeled Handle, Ask and Call. Compare piles with a family member. Where did you disagree? Why?</p>
        <p><b>Say the words.</b> Practice one sentence out loud: “I am not sure about this. Can you help me?” Say it three times until it feels easy.</p>
        <p><b>Go up one bucket.</b> Pick a situation you were unsure about. Which bucket felt right, and which bucket would be safer?</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>You see fire, smoke, a gas smell or something sparking. Leave the place first, then get an adult and call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>Someone is badly hurt or will not wake up. Call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>A job needs a ladder, a sharp blade, a hot stove, a power tool or electricity, and no adult has said yes. Stop and ask.</li>
          <li>You are not sure which bucket it goes in. Ask an adult. If you cannot reach one and someone may be in danger, call 911.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 2</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Your people</h2>
      <p class="sub">Naming the adults you can go to at home, school and nearby.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>Asking is easy when you already know who to ask. A trusted helper can be a family member, a neighbor, a teacher, a security guard, or someone nearby who can help.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> If you make your list before you need it, you will not have to think hard when something goes wrong.</p>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>A busy adult is not a missing adult.</b> Parents and caregivers work, drive, sit in meetings and have dead phone batteries. That does not mean they do not care. It just means you may need to try the next person on your list.</div></div>

      <h3 class="h3">Build your list</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>At home.</b> Write the names of the adults who live with you or look after you, like a parent, a grandparent, an aunt, an older cousin or a caregiver.</li>
        <li><b>At school.</b> Pick two adults you could go to, like your teacher, the office staff, the nurse or a counselor. Know where to find them.</li>
        <li><b>Nearby.</b> Pick a neighbor, a relative or a family friend who lives close and who your family trusts. Ask a parent first. A good nearby helper is someone your family has already said is okay.</li>
        <li><b>Add the numbers.</b> Next to each name, write a phone number you can reach them at.</li>
        <li><b>Keep it where you can find it.</b> Many families post the list near the phone and keep a copy in a backpack.<sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup><sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">Know these by heart</h3>
      <p>FEMA says you should always know four things.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Practice saying each one out loud.</p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Your full name.</b></li>
        <li><b>Your home address.</b> You need it to tell a 911 operator where to send help.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup><sup class="c"><a href="#s5">5</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>The names of your parents or caregivers.</b></li>
        <li><b>Important phone numbers.</b> A parent’s cell phone, a neighbor and a grandparent.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> You can also write these on a card and keep it in your backpack.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="h3">If the first adult is not available</h3>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Try the next name on your list.</b> Go down the list in order. It is okay to try more than one.</li>
        <li><b>Leave a clear message.</b> “This is Sam. I need help with something. Please call me back at this number.” Say it slowly.</li>
        <li><b>Go to the nearest safe adult.</b> At school, that is the office or a teacher. At home, it is the trusted neighbor your family named.</li>
        <li><b>Think about texting.</b> When phone lines are busy, a text can get through when a call does not. FEMA suggests picking one contact for everyone in the family and texting them.<sup class="c"><a href="#s4">4</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>If someone is in danger, do not keep waiting.</b> Move to the third bucket and call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Make a contact card.</b> With a parent, write your full name, address, your parents’ names and three phone numbers on an index card. Put it in your backpack.</p>
        <p><b>Say it three ways.</b> Say your address out loud to a family member. Then say it again slower. Then say it with no help.</p>
        <p><b>Walk the list.</b> Ask a family member to quiz you. “What if Dad does not pick up? Who is next?”</p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>You cannot reach anyone on your list and someone is hurt or in danger. Call 911.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>A person who is not on your list tells you to come with them or keep a secret. Say no, walk away and tell an adult you trust.</li>
          <li>Someone on the phone or at the door asks if you are home alone. Do not say yes. Tell an adult.</li>
          <li>Your list is out of date, like a number changed. Tell a parent so you can fix it.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <span class="num">Lesson 3</span>
      <h2 class="h2">Emergencies</h2>
      <p class="sub">When to leave, when to call 911, and what to say.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Why it matters</h3>
      <p>An emergency is a time when something serious happens and immediate help is needed.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Emergencies are scary. The good news is that you can learn what to do before one happens, so your body knows the plan.</p>

      <h3 class="h3">Leave first</h3>
      <p>The most important thing in an emergency is to stay safe.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> You might see, hear or smell fire, smoke, gasoline, broken glass, flooding, a downed power line or weapons.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> If you are not in a safe place, leave the area, even if someone else is hurt. Once you are safe, you can help by getting help.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>

      <h3 class="h3">Call 911</h3>
      <p>Call 911 when someone is badly hurt, when someone will not wake up, or when you need the police or the fire department.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Do not call for a small cut or bruise you can take care of at home.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>Get to a safe place.</b> Then take a deep breath.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Call 911.</b> Speak slowly and clearly.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Say what is happening.</b> “My neighbor fell and is not moving.”<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Say where you are.</b> At home, give your address. Away from home, give the address or a nearby landmark.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Answer the questions.</b> The operator may ask where you are, what the emergency is, whether the person is awake and breathing, and what number they can use to call you back.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Stay on the line.</b> Do not hang up until the operator says it is okay.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <div class="fact"><div><b>Called by accident?</b> Do not hang up. Stay on the line and tell the operator what happened. If you hang up, they will need to send someone to check on you.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></div></div>

      <h3 class="h3">If there is a fire</h3>
      <p>Get out and stay out. Your family plan should name a meeting place a safe distance from your home.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> Pick something specific, like a tree, a mailbox or the end of the driveway.</p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><b>When the smoke alarm beeps, go.</b> Get out of the house quickly and meet at your safe spot, even if no adult is near.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Get low.</b> Smoke is thinner near the floor. Use your usual way out, or your second way if the first is blocked.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Check doors with the back of your hand.</b> If a door is hot, do not open it. Use another way out.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Stay out.</b> Never go back inside a burning building, not for a pet or a toy.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
        <li><b>Call 911 from outside.</b> Once you are at the meeting place, call 911 and keep everyone close until firefighters arrive.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
      </ol>

      <div class="panel sage try box">
        <h3>Try it at home</h3>
        <p><b>Pretend 911.</b> With an adult, role-play a call. One of you is the operator and asks the questions above. Do not use a real phone for this.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
        <p><b>Fire drill.</b> Walk out of your home with your family, by two different ways if you can. Walk to your meeting place and name it out loud. USFA says everyone in the home should also know how to call 911 and give the address.<sup class="c"><a href="#s2">2</a></sup></p>
        <p><b>Deep breaths.</b> Breathe in for a count of three and out for a count of three. Do it five times. It is a tool you can use any time you feel scared.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></p>
      </div>

      <div class="panel stop box white">
        <h3>Stop and get an adult</h3>
        <ul class="plain">
          <li>You see or smell smoke or fire. Get out and stay out, then call 911 from a safe place.<sup class="c"><a href="#s3">3</a></sup></li>
          <li>Someone is badly hurt or will not wake up. Call 911 and get an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>You smell gas or see a downed power line, flooding or leaking fluid. Stay far away, leave the area and tell an adult.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>You feel too scared or shaky to think. Take deep breaths, get to a safe place and find an adult. Talk about your feelings afterward.<sup class="c"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup></li>
          <li>You are not sure if it is an emergency. Ask an adult. If no adult is near and someone may be in danger, call 911.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
